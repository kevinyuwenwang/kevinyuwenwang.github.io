The Story

Growing up in Shanghai, I was the kid in the principal's office most weeks, either for disrupting class or for the side hustles. By thirteen I was selling jailbroken iPhone apps to classmates. At fifteen my parents sent me to a small academy in the Denver suburbs for a change of scenery.

The first year I was lost. I had learned English from textbooks, and I did not know how to navigate a school where everyone had known each other since kindergarten. In junior year, I took up basketball and soccer while being overweight, ran for hours after school, and went on a diet. Within one year, I lost over fifty pounds. The discipline carried into the classroom, and I developed an interest in finance and served as student treasurer.

College is where that interest got a name and a cost. I opened a brokerage account the moment I turned eighteen and started on the learning curve. Dividends were the first hook. I bought Macy's just to feel like I was getting paid for doing nothing. Then Bitcoin, because I was getting tired of waiting and wanted fast price movement. Then I followed YouTube trading gurus and spent hours hunting for chart patterns and day-trade setups. Then leverage, ten times, twenty times, and for a while it felt effortless. A Tuesday whipsaw wiped the account. I got the margin call mid-trip, skipped the hotel, slept in the car, trying to figure out what went wrong. A few months later I tried options. One hundred times leverage. Gone faster.

I was chasing the next win instead of understanding the mechanism. But things changed on an innocuous junior-year afternoon when I picked up Optimal Mean Reversion Trading by accident while browsing. The math was a foreign language, but I thought maybe this level of mathematics separates a trader from a gambler. I added a mathematics minor halfway through college. My parents thought I had lost my mind.

Graduate school made the career question unavoidable. My classmates knew exactly why they were there and were competing for the same finance internships, while I was half in, half out, summoned here by my parents' order. AI had just started gaining traction, and after spending months chatting with a large language model, skilled trades emerged as the obvious fit for where I was heading.
